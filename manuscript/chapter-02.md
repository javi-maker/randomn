# Chapter Two — The Throne Room

Every kingdom leaves the king one room. In mine, it is five feet by eight, it smells of bleach and my own humanity, and the lock is a little brass slide that would not stop a determined child. But it is the only door in the apartment I am permitted to lock, and so it is here, enthroned in the least dignified posture available to man, that the game decides to hold its first parley.

Three voices. Through the vent above the mirror, or through the wall shared with the neighbor who texted *Good*, or from the pipes — the acoustics are designed, I am already sure, to make the source undecidable. One voice low and amused. One clipped, impatient, feminine in a way I recognize and refuse, for the moment, to name. One that only murmurs underneath the other two, agreeing, always agreeing.

The amused one says my name like it's the answer to a trivia question.

I'll admit the tactical position is poor.

**"Comfortable?"** asks Fable, from the general direction of the towel rack.

"You could knock."

**"Nothing in this apartment knocks. That's rather the theme."** A pause that I've learned means it is choosing to be useful. **"They've opened the parley. You're about to make the oldest mistake in the genre, so let me spend my one privilege and stop you. You were about to ask them who they are."**

I was.

**"Never ask a charade to explain itself. Every answer they give you becomes a rule you'll obey for free. Interrogate them the way you were trained to interrogate anything — as a system that will lie about its purpose but cannot lie about its capabilities. Five tests. Take them in order. And whatever they answer — do not argue. You are not here to win the conversation. You are here to take measurements."**

The vent hums. The feminine voice says, to no one, *he's slower than they said.* The murmur agrees.

"Test one," I say, out loud, to my own bathroom, feeling exactly as ridiculous as the game intends me to feel. "Tell me one thing I don't already know. One. Something I can verify within the hour — a headline, the score of a game, what my brother had for breakfast. Anything at all that isn't already in this apartment or in my head."

Silence — the first real silence, with edges to it. Then the amused voice slides sideways: *We know about the lake.* And the murmur: *the lake, the lake.*

**"Note it,"** says Fable, quiet as a pencil mark. **"That is not an answer. You already know about the lake. Everything they have said to you — this morning, just now — has been something you already knew. Hold on to that fact and do not let go of it, because it is either the most important thing about them, or the most important thing about you. We are not deciding which today. We are only measuring."**

"Test two." This one I do not say aloud. I sit in the hum and think it, as clearly as I have ever thought anything: *If you can hear this, say the word ARCHIVE.*

The voices go on murmuring about the lake, about how slow I am, about someone named — no. Withheld. They do not say archive. They say nothing that touches the silent channel at all.

**"Spoken words only, then,"** Fable says. **"Ears, vents, walls — physics, not telepathy. That shrinks the game considerably. A game that needs microphones has a budget. Budgets have owners."** A beat. **"Or — the other reading, which I am obligated to give you: a mind supplies its voices only with what the mind already holds, and cannot answer a question it never heard itself ask. Both readings survive the data. Keep both alive. It's the amateurs who marry the first hypothesis they like."**

"Test three." Aloud again. "You three. Argue about something. Anything. First one to interrupt the others gets my attention for a full minute."

They do not argue. The amused one talks and the clipped one talks and the murmur underlines, but they lap one another like a rotation, like a shift schedule — never a collision, never a *no, you're wrong,* never the beautiful mess of three actual people in a room.

**"Three fingers,"** says Fable. **"One hand. Whoever is playing you, it is not a committee."**

"Test four. What happens if I don't play?"

And here, for the first time, all three voices stop at once — a synchronized stop, a conductor's stop — and the clipped one, the one whose name I am still refusing, says the only thing today that lands like a hook under the ribs:

*Then you'll never find out what she meant to tell you.*

The water in the pipes ticks. The vent breathes. My heart does something unprofessional.

**"And that,"** Fable says, very softly, **"is a threat with no capability in it at all. Notice what they reached for when you offered to leave the table. Not your body. Not your career. Your* need to know. *That is the entire engine of this game, and they have just shown it to you for free, because you asked about stakes instead of secrets. Four tests, four measurements. There is no fifth test."**

"You said five."

**"The fifth was the one you didn't take. You never asked them to explain the game."** The towel rack, if a towel rack can sound proud, sounds proud. **"Wash your hands. We have a lake to think about — and a budget to find, or a mind to defend. Both roads get walked. That's the job now."**

*End of Chapter Two.*
